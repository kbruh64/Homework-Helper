What makes Pebble different

A tutor that holds back the answer.

Most tools either answer instantly or just track what’s due. Pebble works the way a good teacher does — small questions, small wins, then the leap.

01 — Hint ladder

Seven gentle steps before the solution.

Each wrong attempt unlocks one notch lower. The full answer only appears if every other path is exhausted.

01 Read the question again
02 What do you already know?
03 What is the very first step?
04 Try drawing it out
05 Here’s a similar problem
02 — Live read

Spots stuck vs. guessing.

Reads the rhythm of attempts. Slow tries get a hint. Wild guesses get a re-read.

03 — Streaks that matter

Effort, not speed.

Counts showing up — not finishing fastest. A miss never resets it.

04 — Reading-grade tuned

Words a third-grader knows.

Eight words a sentence. Active voice. No "calculate" or "determine" — just kid words that move the work forward.

05 — Parent digest

A weekly note, not a dashboard.

One paragraph on Friday. What clicked, what tripped, what to ask about at dinner.

How a session feels

Three moods kids walk through.

We designed Pebble around the actual emotional ride of doing homework after a long day — not a tidy abstraction of it.

01 — The start

Tense and tired.

I don’t even know where to start.

Pebble starts with one tiny step. Not the answer. Not even the strategy — just one small move so the brain stops freezing.

02 — The shift

Curious and thinking.

Wait, do I add or multiply?

One step in and they’re asking their own questions. Pebble answers questions with questions, gently — until the answer is theirs.

03 — The land

Proud and lit up.

Oh — I got it!

When it clicks, Pebble celebrates the specific move they made. Not "good job" — but "you remembered to find a common denominator first."

How a session goes

Five minutes from stuck to "oh, I see."

No setup, no log-ins for the kid, no quiz to prove they need it. They open it, type the problem, and Pebble takes it from there.

001

They type the problem.

Or take a photo, or paste it in. Pebble reads it back in plain language to confirm.

002

Pebble asks first.

"What do you think the first step is?" — every session starts the same way. The student does the first move, even if it’s tiny.

003

Hints walk down.

Wrong tries unlock smaller hints. Right tries get specific praise — what they did, not just that they did it.

004

The win lands.

When the answer comes, Pebble offers a similar one to lock it in. No badge for being fast — only for finishing the thinking.
